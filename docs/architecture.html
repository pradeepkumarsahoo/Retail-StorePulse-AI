v0.1 · Prototype
AI-Powered Store Operations · Workforce Intelligence · Decision Platform

From what happened to what we should do, with evidence a manager can trust.

StorePulse-AI is a decision-support platform for multi-store retailers. It unifies fragmented store, workforce, and operations data into governed analytics and explains the signals through constrained agentic workflows — every recommendation carries its evidence, its assumptions, and a human in the loop.

01 Problem Statement

Eight fragmentation points that quietly cost every store.

Large multi-store retailers run on data that never meets in one place. A regional manager overseeing 40 stores typically touches 6+ systems before 9am — POS, workforce management, inventory, labor-budget sheets, exception emails, BI dashboards — then reconciles the gaps in a spreadsheet. The data exists. The decision window does not. Each day, measurable revenue and labor dollars leak through eight recurring fractures in the operating model.

Visibility

Fragmented view across systems

Sales, labor, footfall, and inventory signals live in separate systems with different refresh cadences, grain, and owners. Managers reconcile them in spreadsheets, so no one sees a trusted, unified store picture before the decisions of the day are already made.

Reactive

Descriptive, not diagnostic

Dashboards report what happened — not why. Which drivers moved and what to do about it falls to manual investigation — tribal knowledge, side-conversations, and gut feel — so the same questions are re-asked every week across stores and regions.

Workforce

Static staffing plans miss real demand

Schedules built on weekly averages miss hourly and intra-day demand. Peak periods run short on coverage and lose conversion; trough periods carry excess hours. Labor productivity and customer service erode together, and neither is visible until payroll closes.

Late Signals

Anomalies caught too late

Conversion drops, availability gaps, basket-size shifts, and refund spikes surface in month-end reviews — long after corrective action is cheap. By the time a store manager learns about an exception, the week, the promotion, or the season is already gone.

Trust

KPIs that disagree across teams

Three teams, three versions of "sales per labor hour." Metric definitions, filters, and time-window rules differ by tool, so decision cycles stall on definitional debates instead of action. Confidence in the data — and in any AI built on top of it — collapses the moment numbers conflict.

Inbound Blindspot

Labor arrives, inventory doesn't

Associates clock in on schedule, but the DC truck is late, re-routed, or short — so there is nothing to stock, face, or move to the floor. Labor laws make it costly to send staff home once they've reported, and front-office tasks can't absorb a full crew, so paid hours convert to idle time. Without live inbound-truck ETAs from distribution centers, shift plans cannot flex with arrival variance, and millions of dollars a year leak out as unproductive labor hours — a silent tax no dashboard shows today.

External Signals

Forecasts ignore the real world

Weekly sales and labor forecasts are built almost entirely on internal history — last year, last month, last four weeks — and stay blind to the forces that will actually shape next week's footfall: severe weather, heat waves, snow events, political protests and curfews, regional conflict, strikes, transit disruptions, holidays observed differently by community, viral social trends, and local event cancellations. Planners scan news and social feeds informally, triage by gut, and either over-staff defensively or get caught short when the signal was already in the public record days earlier. The miss cuts both ways — lost sales on surge days, paid idle labor on collapse days — and neither is traceable to a forecast assumption anyone can audit.

Customer Sentiment

Stores are blind to how customers feel

Service score comes from occasional NPS surveys with single-digit response rates, lagging the actual experience by weeks. The frustrated shopper at checkout, the confused browser at the service desk, the abandoned cart at a chaotic aisle — none of it reaches the manager in time to coach, adjust, or recover. By the time dissatisfaction shows up in a review, a return, or a churn number, the moment is gone: the associate didn't know to engage, the shift lead didn't know to re-staff, and the same pattern repeats the next day in the same zones at the same hours. Managers are left asking "were we attentive today?" with no evidence to answer.

Business impact

The compounded effect is missed sales, avoidable operating expense, poor labor productivity, long customer waits, and inconsistent execution across the fleet — a gap that widens every quarter the operating model stays spreadsheet-bound.

02 Proposed Solution

A governed decision layer, not another dashboard.

Core promise

Move store teams from "what happened?" to "what's likely next, why, and what should we consider doing" — with every claim traceable to its data, its time window, and the reviewer who approved it.

StorePulse-AI is a decision-support platform — not an autonomous scheduler or employee-management authority. It unifies store, workforce, and operations data into a governed analytics core, then layers constrained agentic workflows that explain the signals and propose next actions. Nine working capabilities ship on a single modular monolith: deterministic where accuracy demands it (KPIs, forecasts, anomaly thresholds), AI-assisted where interpretation adds value (hypothesis ranking, briefings, drill-down questions). Every operational recommendation routes through a human reviewer before anything moves in a source system.

Analytics

Regional command center

Unified KPIs across stores, regions, and districts with a central metric catalog, lineage from source event to dashboard tile, and per-metric validation status. One definition of sales per labor hour, conversion, shrink, and basket, enforced for both humans and agents.

Forecasting

Workforce demand

Store · department · time-slot labor forecasts with baseline comparison, holdout backtests, WAPE/MAE reporting, and a coverage-gap view that flags where scheduled hours diverge from predicted demand — before the schedule is published.

Detection

Anomaly & exception feed

Rules plus statistical detection on sales, labor, conversion, returns, availability, and refunds. Each flag carries its baseline, deviation, confidence band, and the exact query that produced it, so the manager can trust it without opening another tool.

Agentic

Evidence-first investigation

Specialist agents (sales, labor, inventory, customer-experience) retrieve validated metrics through a typed tool gateway, rank competing hypotheses, and cite the records, time windows, and calculations behind every claim. No free-text speculation, no hidden chain of thought.

Briefing

Daily manager briefing

Role-specific morning digest for store managers, regional leads, and workforce planners: priorities for the day, workforce observations, open anomalies, and suggested next actions — each actionable in one click, each traceable back to its source evidence.

Inbound

DC & truck tracking

Live visibility of outbound loads from each distribution center — truck ID, SKUs on board, planned departure, GPS/telematics ETA, and delay reasons — joined to the store's shift plan. The system continuously re-projects "stockable work hours" per store per day, flags shifts where predicted labor supply will outrun stockable workload, and recommends pre-approved reassignments (training, resets, back-of-house, customer recovery) so paid hours stay productive when a truck slips.

External Signals

Weather, events & news sensing

A governed external-signal layer ingests weather forecasts (NOAA / national met services), public event calendars, transit and traffic advisories, and a curated feed of news and social-media trends filtered by store trade-area. Signals are scored for relevance, blast-radius, and confidence, then surfaced as explicit forecast adjusters: each weekly sales and labor projection carries the baseline number, the adjustment delta, the triggering signal, and its source link. A reviewer can accept, override, or reject the adjuster, and every published forecast records the exact external evidence it used — so defensive over-staffing and missed surge days both become auditable decisions, not gut calls.

Service Experience

Camera sentiment & daily service score

Opt-in, privacy-preserving computer vision at entrance, checkout, and service-desk zones reads aggregated, anonymized facial-sentiment signals (positive · neutral · frustrated · distressed) and rolls them into a per-store daily service score with hourly and zone-level breakdowns. The system never identifies individuals, stores no raw frames (edge inference → feature vectors only), honors regional law (GDPR / BIPA / state biometric statutes), requires consent signage, and is disabled by default per store.

Each morning briefing surfaces: yesterday's service score vs. baseline, zones and hours where sentiment dropped, a correlated view against queue length, staff count, truck arrivals, and weather, and a coaching nudge for the shift lead — e.g. "Checkout trended frustrated 4–6pm; consider opening a second register and a floor-walker." Detail drill-down shows a minute-by-minute sentiment curve with linked events. Scores measure customer experience in zones and time windows, never individual employees; used to help associates give better attention, not to rank them.

Governance

Audit & approvals

Model versions, data-quality snapshots, run traces, tool-call logs, prompt and policy versions, and a recommendation lifecycle (drafted → reviewed → approved → acted → outcome) that requires explicit human sign-off and preserves the full evidence trail for auditors.

How it maps to the problem

Visibility → unified KPI service. Reactive → agentic root-cause investigation. Workforce → time-slot demand forecasts with coverage gaps. Late signals → continuous anomaly detection feeding the daily briefing. Trust → one metric catalog, lineage, and model/version provenance on every claim. Inbound blindspot → DC & truck tracking joined to the shift plan, so idle hours are predicted and redeployed before the day starts. External signals → weather, events, and curated news/social feeds become explicit, auditable adjusters on the weekly sales and labor forecast. Customer sentiment → opt-in camera sentiment rolled into a daily service score, by zone and hour, with coaching nudges for shift leads — never individual employee scoring.

03 Architecture

A modular monolith with clean boundaries and guarded agents.

One deployable, four logical layers, strict trust boundaries around the AI runtime. Domain logic stays independent of FastAPI, React, the LLM provider, and the MCP adapter — so any of them can be swapped without rewriting the core.

04 Agentic Workflow

Agents retrieve. Humans decide.

No agent touches the database directly. The supervisor classifies intent, allocates a step and token budget, and dispatches specialists that can only call allowlisted, typed tools. Output is schema-validated and evidence-checked before a human reviewer sees it.

06 Guardrails

Principles that shape every build decision.

These are not aspirations. They are tests the implementation must pass at each milestone — and they constrain what the agents are even capable of doing.

01

Deterministic where it counts

SQL, KPI math, and validation are code — not LLM calls. LLMs summarize verified evidence, never invent calculations.

02

Evidence or silence

Every finding cites the metric, window, and source. If evidence is insufficient, the system says so instead of guessing.

03

Read-only by default

No schedule, employee, or payroll mutation in v1. Recommendations are proposals routed to a human reviewer.

04

Least-privilege tools

Agents reach the world only through a typed, allowlisted gateway. Tool descriptions aren't security — authorization is.

05

Responsible workforce AI

No individual employee scoring, ranking, or surveillance. Forecasts estimate workload, not punish people.

06

Honest status

Synthetic data is labeled synthetic. Opportunity estimates are labeled estimates. Compliance claims require independent assessment.

07 Stack

Boring, maintained, pinned.

Chosen for support and security over novelty. Versions are pinned; additions require a note in docs/DECISIONS.md with purpose and alternatives.
